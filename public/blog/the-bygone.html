the bygone

August 30, 2026

CW: depression, suicide

The only remaining proof of years of my life is stored in a folder of markdown files in a hard drive. Last night, I decided to revisit my notes stored in my phone from yesteryear. I didn't think much of it then (it was only supposed to take a couple minutes), since my goal was just to archive my writing and future-proof the file formats with a new program. I couldn't have prepared myself for what was coming.

I'd always thought of myself as pretty reserved, holding my emotions close to myself and processing them internally. I know that I used to journal when I was really little, and that I brought that habit with me when I got my first phone. I know that the last time I journaled in my phone was sometime in early high school. Unfortunately, that's about all I remember. I've forgotten a lot. I don't remember what I wrote, or who I was when I wrote them. I was pretty certain I only had a few entries, but clearly, I misremembered that too.

I stayed awake far longer than I intended to just skimming through all of it—almost four years worth, each entry being hundreds or thousands of words long. Some read like a story while others were written in poetic rhythm. Some were split into cohesive paragraphs while even more were unbroken sheets of text that seemed to stretch on forever. I have no memory of writing most of them.

It felt as if I were reading someone else's diary. Snooping in places that didn't belong to me. I was peeking over the shoulder of a bygone author who had so much to say about their loneliness, their fear of the world, and what they hoped might one day happen. They wrote letters addressed to god-knows-who, and prose intended to spill into and flood the pits of hell. I found a suicide note, and then I found more. It just kept on going and going.

It destroyed me to accept that the bygone author could only be myself. Here laid years of my life that came and passed, and the only evidence that I experienced any of it were these sorrow-filled essays I left behind.

There's a fear that has arisen that there could be more of my life that I don't know about. Maybe I missed some, deleted some, or wrote about it in notebooks and scattered papers collecting dust in a location I've forgotten. I feel grief and regret at the likelihood that many things, feelings, and aspects of my life never had the chance to be put into words that I can read now. I know the author's name, but I don't know who they were. I can't recall how they really felt. I can only take their word for it.

Maybe the author was writing to me.
