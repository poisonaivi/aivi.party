world wide webbing

I published my first website in February 2024 2024-02-22. At the time, I had no clue what I was doing. I learned some HTML in school, then monkey-on-typewriter'd and flipped through forum discussions until my first static site was accessible on the public Internet. It was arguably a pretty ugly-looking thing, but I loved the process and I couldn't stop thinking of the possibilities.


Eight months of experimentation later 2024-10-19, I landed on a design that ultimately became the foundation of the site today. A black background, two central columns, and everything filtered through some CSS mask wizardry that made the page appear like a CRT screen. I finally decided to spend the twelve bucks on a nicer-sounding domain.


An entire year 2026-01-06 of small changes and upgrades passed before the next major revision, but I decided to buy a new domain, aivi.party, and start working on an experimental recode using Cloudflare Workers and a custom API. I also decided I would start a blog, and I've worked on that since.


I'd always been pretty fond of short hacked domain names. Sometimes, for fun, I would search registrars for available domains. Manually searching, however, was too slow and too inexhaustive to find anything cool. In October 2026 2026-10-08, a script I wrote to scrape available unique hacked domains found poi.sn. I bought it right then and there.


My original intent for making this website is a bit unclear. Maybe as a form of escapism, searching for popularity, or reflecting on my existence. At some point in the process, I found the indie web and truly saw the Internet for what it could be. Not news outlets, not social media, not stock markets, not online games, but just the foundational capabilties of the World Wide Web and the huge space it offers to make whatever the hell you want. The information-transporting systems enmeshing our planet provide so many paths to creativity, and there's a global community of people who've been building on it for decades. With all this beauty hidden behind the scenes, I'm just really happy that I went out of my way to look for it.
